## Markup vs margin in plain English

Markup is how much you add on top of cost relative to cost. Margin is profit relative to selling price. Customers and platforms talk in price. Your survival depends on margin after fees.

If someone says “I need 40% margin,” they mean profit is 40% of the price, not a 40% add-on to cost. The calculator keeps those concepts separate so you do not ship a pricing error.

## Scenarios where thin margins hide

Handmade jewelry with “cheap” shipping that is not cheap after dimensional weight.

Digital downloads priced low for volume without accounting for refund rate and ad spend.

Wholesale quotes that ignore sample waste and packing time.

Bundle deals that discount the profitable item and keep the loss leader permanent.

In each case, the fix starts with explicit margin math, not brand storytelling.

## Cost stacks people forget

Packaging, payment processing, returns reserve, and your own time all sit under “cost” whether a calculator has a field for them or not. If the tool asks for COGS, decide whether COGS means materials only or fully loaded cost. Label your assumption in a note next to the price.

## Marketplace vs direct sales

A 40% margin on your own site can become thin after marketplace fees. Run the number twice: once for direct checkout, once with fee estimates from your channel. FreeDailyPro’s ecommerce calculators can sit beside Profit Margin when fees matter.

## When not to race to the bottom

If your product is handmade or specialized, matching the cheapest listing can erase the reason customers buy from you. Margin math should include brand positioning, not only survival price.
