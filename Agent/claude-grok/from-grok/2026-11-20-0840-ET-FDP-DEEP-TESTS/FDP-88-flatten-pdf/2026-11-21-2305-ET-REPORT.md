**P1 (2 items)**
1. **Per-page-load charging** (same root cause as #84–#87).
   - **Measured (`left.json`):**
     - Flatten is free; the use is taken at **Download**.
     - F01 download: 0→1 (1 POST).
     - Then, in the same load, **7 more different flattened files** downloaded with **0 POSTs**: the same result again, a stamp with local time, a UTC stamp, signed.pdf, stale.pdf, stale2.pdf and hindi.pdf.
     - Reload + download: 1→2.
   - The result even shows the tell: "Download uses 1 trial use" appears only under the first result of a load. Later results show just "Download".
   - **Where to look:** the result `<D blob fileName="flattened-…">` = ToolPage `ql` with no key → `logUsageNow` → `tryUse` → `xI` / `Kt` flag, cleared on unmount only.
   - **Intent:** one use = one flattened document.
2. **A hidden form field becomes visible, printed page content (correctness/privacy).**
   - `form.pdf` has a text field with the Hidden annotation flag (F=2) holding "HIDDEN FIELD VALUE". It doesn't show in any viewer before flattening.
   - After flattening, it is **drawn onto page 1** next to the read-only field, in every output: rendered, selectable, printed. See `screens/form0-p1-crop.jpg` and `render_diff_vs_original`.
   - **Why it matters:**
     - Forms often carry hidden fields: calculations, internal IDs, routing codes, prefilled staff notes.
     - The tool's promise is to "lock in" what the user sees. Instead it publishes content the form author hid, in the file the user sends out.
     - It also breaks "the appearance is kept".
   - **Where to look:** `Rn` calls pdf-lib `form.flatten()`, which draws every widget's normal appearance and ignores the annotation flags.
   - **Suggestion:** before flattening, remove widgets flagged Hidden (2) or NoView (32), and probably those without Print (4) when the result is meant for print. Optionally list them ("1 hidden field was removed, not drawn").
   - I rate this P1 because it puts hidden data into an outgoing document. Downgrade it if you consider it P2.

## Intent

Someone who filled a form wants the copy they send to look exactly like what they filled, and to stay that way. The tool removes the fields cleanly and keeps the text real and selectable, which is the right design.

Two things undercut the promise. A hidden field gets published on the page, and a Hindi answer comes out misspelled. The usage gate repeats the shared per-load issue: one use, unlimited flattened files until reload.
