*Screenshots with highlights beat long email descriptions*

[FreeDailyPro.com](https://www.freedailypro.com) -- “It looks wrong on my screen” wastes hours. A marked-up screenshot shows the exact button, error text, and page state. Your developer friend—or your future self—can act without a twenty-message thread.

This post is about capture plus simple annotation. The tool is FreeDailyPro’s [Screenshot Tool](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/screenshot-tool).

## What good bug screenshots include

Visible URL if relevant, full error text, and a highlight on the control that failed. Blur personal data. Crop away noise.

## Workflow

1. Capture the screen at the moment of the issue.
2. Annotate with [Screenshot Tool](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/screenshot-tool)—highlight, arrow, blur secrets.
3. Export and attach to the ticket or email.
4. For multi-step guides, collect frames into [Image to PDF](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/image-to-pdf).

See [multitool apps](https://www.freedailypro.com/multitool-apps).

## How-to notes for family tech support

Number steps. One screenshot per step. Avoid walls of unannotated desktop photos.

## Honest limits

Does not replace full session recording for complex bugs. Does not capture issues that never appear on screen. Blur sensitive data before you send.

## Closing

Clear pictures shorten troubleshooting. FreeDailyPro’s [Screenshot Tool](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/screenshot-tool) helps you capture, mark, and share what you see. Highlight the problem, hide secrets, send once.


## Related habits that keep the workflow clean

After you finish with the primary tool, take thirty seconds to file the output where you will find it again. A clear filename and a dated folder beat a desktop full of exports. If you work with a partner, agree once on where final files live so nobody hunts chat history for the “real” version.

When something looks off in the result, fix the source input rather than stacking workarounds. Re-running a clean pass is faster than explaining a messy file to a client or classmate later.


## Privacy and common sense

Only process files and text you are allowed to handle in a browser tool. Payroll, medical, and confidential legal material may require approved systems only—follow your policy even when a free tool is convenient. Close the tab when you are done on a shared computer. Do not leave client data on a screen in a café.

If your organization blocks third-party tools, use the path IT provides. This guide assumes you are allowed to use FreeDailyPro for the task described.


## What “done” looks like

You should leave with a file or a number you can act on: send the PDF, paste the result, or record the figure in your notes. If you cannot state the next action in one sentence, the workflow is not finished. Open [Screenshot Tool](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/screenshot-tool) only when you know what success means for this task.


## Teaching someone else the same steps

If a teammate will repeat this job, write the five steps in your internal doc with the live tool link. Do not screenshot a dozen menus from other products. The value of a narrow browser tool is that the path stays short enough to teach in minutes.
