## Three questions people mix up

1. What is X% of Y? (discount amount from a price)
2. Y is what percent of Z? (share of a total)
3. What is the percent change from A to B? (raise or growth)

Using the wrong formula quietly ships wrong prices and wrong offer letters. The calculator does not care about your story; it cares which fields you fill.

## Retail examples you can reuse

- Discount: original $120, 25% off → discount $30 → sale $90.
- Stacked marketing language: “extra 10% off sale price” is not the same as 35% off original—compute in order.
- Margin conversations: know whether you are talking markup on cost or margin on sell price; they are not interchangeable.

Write the base down every time. Most errors come from applying the percent to the wrong number (sale price vs original, new salary vs old).

## Raise and freelance rate examples

- Salary: $50,000 plus 8% → +$4,000 → $54,000.
- Freelance rate: $85/hour plus 12% → about $95.20/hour.
- Commission: 12% of $8,400 → $1,008.

When you negotiate, keep percent and absolute dollars both visible. An “only 3%” frame hides a meaningful annual amount; a huge-looking percent on a small base can be tiny money.

## Markup versus margin (do not blur them)

People say “20% margin” when they mean markup on cost, and the reverse. Example: cost $80, sell $100.

- Markup on cost: $20 / $80 = 25%
- Margin on price: $20 / $100 = 20%

FreeDailyPro’s [Percentage Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/percentage-calculator) will compute whatever ratio you enter—it will not correct vocabulary. Label your inputs in the notebook beside the keyboard.

## Worked walkthrough: discount then tax (conceptual)

Suppose an item is $80 before a 15% discount, then 8% sales tax applies to the discounted price in your region’s rules (always confirm local tax law—this is arithmetic practice only).

1. 15% of 80 = 12 → discounted price 68.
2. 8% of 68 = 5.44 → total about 73.44.

People often tax the original price by mistake or discount after tax. FreeDailyPro’s [Percentage Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/percentage-calculator) helps each step; sequence still depends on policy.

## Worked walkthrough: percent change for traffic

Last month 1,200 visits, this month 1,500. Percent change = (1500−1200)/1200 = 25% increase. If the funnel conversion rate stayed flat, leads scale with traffic; if not, dig deeper. The percent is the headline, not the full story.

## Avoid calculator theater in meetings

Projecting a calculator while arguing strategy can look like precision without insight. Compute once, state the assumption, move to decisions. Numbers support judgment; they are not a substitute for it.
