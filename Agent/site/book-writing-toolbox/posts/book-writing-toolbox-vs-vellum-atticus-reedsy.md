## Where Vellum is genuinely stronger

Vellum has years of maturity and a reputation for highly polished ebook and print interiors. It is Mac-only, and public materials treat that limitation as firm. Pricing is a one-time purchase commonly listed around $199.99 for ebook-focused licensing and around $249.99 for Press (ebook plus print)—confirm live pricing on Vellum’s store because regional pages and promotions differ.

If you are on a Mac, publish often, and care deeply about a large library of refined interior styles, Vellum remains a rational default. Book Writing Toolbox is not claiming to out-theme a long-built design system overnight.

## Where Atticus is genuinely stronger

Atticus is repeatedly positioned as the cross-platform answer: Windows, Mac, Linux, Chromebook, with browser-friendly access and a built-in writing environment. Public pricing is commonly a one-time $147 for unlimited books and updates—again, verify on atticus.io before purchase.

Authors who want one paid tool for drafting and formatting across devices often land here. Theme depth and product maturity are real competitive strengths relative to newer free tools.

## Where Reedsy Studio is a real free alternative

Reedsy Studio’s core writing and formatting features are free, including export paths vendors describe for EPUB and print PDF. Optional paid add-ons exist for extras such as deeper history, stats, or outlining boards; published figures often cite roughly $4.99 and $7.99 monthly add-ons, but Reedsy’s pricing UI can be dynamic—check current studio pricing pages.

Limits show up as fewer style controls and less typographic fine-tuning than dedicated paid formatters. For a first book on a zero software budget, that trade can still be correct.

## What Book Writing Toolbox actually optimizes for

Book Writing Toolbox is free in the browser with no account required to write or preview. Drafts autosave locally. Preview and Pre-flight are free and unlimited, using real typeset renders and real EPUB builds. The publishing platform picker applies platform-specific trim lists, margin math, and ISBN guidance across KDP, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press, Lulu, and ebook-only targets.

EPUB is always free and unlimited; clean PDF/manuscript downloads use a credit pool ($6.99/1, $17.99/3, $49.99/10). A $3.99 EPUB & Manuscript Pass clears watermarked limits for 24 hours of unlimited clean EPUB + manuscript downloads.

Differentiators worth stating without inflation: multi-platform rule handling, free pre-flight against the selected platform, and a pricing shape that does not require a $100+ license before you can see a real preview.

## Honest trade-offs for Book Writing Toolbox

It is newer than Vellum’s long track record. It will not match the deepest commercial theme libraries of tools that spent years selling interior design as the product. Preview/Pre-flight/Download briefly send content to a server for real typesetting; drafts are local, but you should still follow common-sense privacy practices.

If your only goal is the prettiest possible Mac-bound interior and you already budgeted for Vellum, buy Vellum. If you need Windows + paid all-in-one maturity, Atticus is a fair contender. If you need $0 software with acceptable exports, Reedsy Studio belongs on the list.

## Who should pick what

- Pick Vellum if you are on Mac, want maximum mature styling options, and prefer a one-time pro formatter with a long indie track record.
- Pick Atticus if you need cross-platform access, like writing and formatting in one paid product, and $147 one-time fits your plan.
- Pick Reedsy Studio if you want a free browser editor with free core exports and can live with lighter design control.
- Pick Book Writing Toolbox if you want to write and preview free in the browser, run free pre-flight, keep EPUB free, and format with explicit multi-platform trim/margin/ISBN rules before spending credits on clean print files.

You can also sequence tools: draft anywhere, format where the storefront rules are clearest, pay only when a clean file is due. The worst outcome is paying twice because the first tool ignored the second platform’s margin math.

One more decision filter: count how many storefronts you will upload to in the next twelve months. A single-channel KDP experiment has different needs than a KDP + IngramSpark + ebook wide release. Multi-channel authors should prioritize tools that make platform rule differences visible before payment. Single-channel authors may prioritize interior theme beauty instead. Neither preference is wrong; they solve different bottlenecks.

Whatever you choose, verify current vendor pricing the day you buy. The comparison table above is a field guide, not a contract with each company’s finance team.
