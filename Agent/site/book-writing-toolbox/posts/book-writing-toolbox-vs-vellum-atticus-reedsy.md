## Where Vellum is genuinely stronger

Vellum has years of maturity and a reputation for highly polished ebook and print interiors. It is **Mac-only**, and public materials treat that as firm. Pricing is a one-time purchase commonly listed around **$199.99** (ebooks) and **$249.99** (Press: ebook + print). Confirm live pricing on Vellum’s store—regional pages and promotions differ.

If you are on a Mac, publish often, and want a deep library of refined interior styles, Vellum remains a rational default. Book Writing Toolbox is not pretending to out-theme a long-built design system overnight.

## Where Atticus is genuinely stronger

Atticus is the cross-platform answer writers recommend when Vellum is off the table: **Windows, Mac, Linux, Chromebook**, browser-friendly access, built-in writing environment. Public pricing is commonly a **one-time $147** for unlimited books and updates—verify on atticus.io.

Authors who want one paid tool for drafting and formatting across devices often land here. Theme depth and product maturity are real strengths versus newer free tools.

## Where Reedsy Studio is a real free alternative

Reedsy Studio’s **core writing and formatting are free**, including export paths vendors describe for EPUB and print PDF. Optional paid add-ons exist for extras (deeper history, stats, outlining boards). Published figures often cite roughly **$4.99** and **$7.99** monthly add-ons; Reedsy’s pricing UI can be dynamic—check current pages.

Limits show up as fewer style controls and less typographic fine-tuning than dedicated paid formatters. For a first book on a zero software budget, that trade can still be correct.

## What Book Writing Toolbox actually optimizes for

Book Writing Toolbox runs free in the browser with **no account required to write or preview**. Drafts autosave locally. **Preview and Pre-flight are free and unlimited**, using real typeset renders and real EPUB builds.

The **publishing platform picker** applies platform-specific trim lists, margin math, and ISBN guidance across KDP, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press, Lulu, and ebook-only targets (Draft2Digital, Apple Books, Kobo Writing Life).

Differentiators, without inflation: multi-platform rule handling, free pre-flight against the selected platform, and a pricing shape that does not require a $100+ license before you see a real preview.

## Honest trade-offs

Book Writing Toolbox is newer than Vellum’s long track record. It will not match the deepest commercial theme libraries of tools that spent years selling interior design as the product. Preview/Pre-flight/Download briefly send content to a server for real typesetting; drafts are local, but use common-sense privacy habits.

If your only goal is the prettiest Mac-bound interior and you already budgeted for Vellum, buy Vellum. If you need Windows + paid all-in-one maturity, Atticus is a fair contender. If you need $0 software with acceptable exports, Reedsy Studio belongs on the shortlist.

## Who should pick what

- **Vellum** — Mac user who wants maximum mature styling and a one-time pro formatter with a long indie track record.
- **Atticus** — Needs cross-platform access, likes writing + formatting in one paid product, and $147 one-time fits the plan.
- **Reedsy Studio** — Wants a free browser editor with free core exports and can live with lighter design control.
- **Book Writing Toolbox** — Wants to write and preview free in the browser, run free pre-flight, keep EPUB free, and format with explicit multi-platform trim/margin/ISBN rules before spending credits on clean print files.

You can also sequence tools: draft anywhere, format where storefront rules are clearest, pay only when a clean file is due. The worst outcome is paying twice because the first tool ignored the second platform’s margin math.

Count storefronts for the next twelve months. A single-channel KDP experiment is different from KDP + IngramSpark + wide ebook. Multi-channel authors should prioritize tools that make platform rule differences visible before payment. Single-channel authors may prioritize interior theme beauty instead. Neither preference is wrong.

Also weigh collaboration: if a co-author is on Windows and you are on Mac, Mac-only tooling creates friction even when interiors look beautiful. Browser options reduce that friction at a different design ceiling.

Write down the constraint that actually hurts—OS, budget, multi-storefront rules, or theme polish—before you compare feature checklists. Then verify vendor pricing the day you buy. This table is a field guide, not a contract with each company’s finance team.

## How to decide in one coffee

Write four answers on a sticky note:

1. What OS do I actually use daily?
2. How many storefronts will I upload to this year?
3. Do I need the prettiest interior themes, or do I need correct platform rules first?
4. What is my software budget before the first royalty dollar?

If the sticky note says “Mac + beauty + budget exists,” Vellum is in the lead. If it says “Windows + write and format together + $147,” Atticus is in the lead. If it says “$0 + browser + good enough export,” Reedsy Studio is in the lead. If it says “browser + free preview/pre-flight + multi-platform rules + pay per clean print file,” Book Writing Toolbox is in the lead.
