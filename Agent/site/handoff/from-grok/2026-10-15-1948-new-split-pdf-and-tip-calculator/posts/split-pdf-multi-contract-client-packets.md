## Why one mega-PDF fails client handoff

A combined scan is fine for your archive. It is a poor client deliverable. Recipients open the wrong section, reply about pages that belong to someone else, or ask you to resend "just my agreement" after you already thought the work was done.

Email systems also treat large attachments as risky. A 40-page color scan can sit near attachment caps, especially if the notary used high resolution. Splitting into three smaller packets keeps each message focused and easier to store under the right matter name.

Teams that share a matter folder make the same mistake: they drop the mega-file into a shared drive and hope naming alone prevents mix-ups. Naming helps, but separate files make the structure obvious when a paralegal or bookkeeper opens the folder weeks later.

## Scenario: three clients, one closing day scan

Imagine a freelance closing coordinator. Monday's scan covers:

- Pages 1–12: Client A residential purchase agreement and exhibits  
- Pages 13–27: Client B lease amendment with schedules  
- Pages 28–40: Client C short-form services agreement  

You need three outbound emails tonight. Each client should receive only their pages. Your archive still keeps the original scan for the day.

Before splitting, skim page 12, page 27, and page 40 on a large screen. Confirm the last page of each packet is complete (signatures visible, no cut-off exhibit). If page 13 starts mid-sentence of Client A's exhibit, adjust the range before you export.

## Numbered workflow on Split PDF

1. Open [Split PDF](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/split-pdf) on FreeDailyPro.com.  
2. Upload the multi-contract scan. Stay within your daily allowance of 10 trial usages if you are testing ranges; heavy closing weeks may need a day pass.  
3. Choose page-range mode rather than splitting every page into its own file. For this scenario you want three ranges, not forty singles.  
4. Enter range 1–12, name the output something like `ClientA-purchase-2026-10-22.pdf`.  
5. Enter range 13–27 as `ClientB-lease-amendment-2026-10-22.pdf`.  
6. Enter range 28–40 as `ClientC-services-2026-10-22.pdf`.  
7. Download each packet and open it once before you attach it to email.  
8. Store the original mega-scan in your archive folder labeled with the closing date so you can re-split if a range was wrong.

> Tip: Keep client names in the file name only if your privacy policy allows it; some teams use matter codes instead.

If a packet still feels heavy for email after the split, run that single file through [Compress PDF](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/compress-pdf) before you send. Compress after split, not before, so you do not blur signatures across packets you never needed to touch.

## Worked example with realistic page counts

Source file: `closing-day-2026-10-22-scan.pdf`, 40 pages, about 18 MB.

After split:

- Client A: 12 pages, about 5.1 MB  
- Client B: 15 pages, about 6.8 MB  
- Client C: 13 pages, about 5.9 MB  

Those sizes are still large for some mobile mail apps. If Client B's schedules are photo-heavy, compress only Client B until the attachment is under your firm's usual send limit. Leave Client A alone if signatures look crisp.

Re-check page 1 of each download: it should start with that client's cover or signature block, not leftover text from the previous matter. Re-check the last page for a complete signature block and any notary stamp that belongs to that packet only.

## Common mistakes when splitting client packets

**Off-by-one ranges.** People type 1–13 when page 13 is already Client B. Fix by previewing the first page of the second range before export.

**Splitting every page.** Forty one-page files create chaos in email and in matter folders. Use ranges unless a court or portal literally requires one page per upload.

**Sending the mega-file "just this once."** That is how Client C receives Client A's bank details if exhibits were scanned together. Prefer the extra two minutes of splitting.

**Compressing the whole stack first.** Global compression can reduce quality on pages you will never send. Split first, compress only the heavy packet.

**Skipping a visual open.** Download is not the same as open. Always open the file and scroll the first and last page before attach.

## Honest limits

Split PDF cannot invent missing pages. If the notary skipped page 19, you still need a re-scan. The tool also does not redline or retype contracts; it only divides what is already in the file. Extremely large scans may hit browser memory limits on older machines—if the tab freezes, try a machine with more RAM or compress a duplicate of the source first for the split step only, then archive the full-quality original separately.

Files are processed in the browser for this workflow so the multi-contract scan is not stored as a FreeDailyPro cloud matter file. Still follow your own retention rules when you save downloads to disk or matter systems.
