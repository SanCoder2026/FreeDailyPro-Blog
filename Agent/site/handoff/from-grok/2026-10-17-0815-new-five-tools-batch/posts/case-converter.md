## Pick a house style and stick to it

Title Case For Marketing Headlines is common in US product sites. sentence case for long help articles is common in docs. ALL CAPS for a single CTA can work; ALL CAPS for paragraphs harms reading. Decide once per surface (site, email, PDF) and convert toward that rule.

## What to paste (and what to leave out)

Paste the heading list or the paragraph you own. Leave code blocks, product SKUs that require exact casing, and legal defined terms that must match a contract. After convert, skim proper nouns: iPhone, FreeDailyPro, and client names often need manual fixes because automatic title case mangles brand spelling.

## Small words in title case

Style guides disagree on capitalizing "a," "to," "of," and "the" in the middle of titles. Pick a guide (or your brand book) and apply it consistently. Automatic converters may not match your exception list—budget thirty seconds for manual fixes on hero lines.

## Common mistakes

Title-casing an entire blog post body. Uppercasing paragraphs for "emphasis." Converting legal party names that must match a filing. Slugging before final case fixes, then wondering why the URL encodes odd capitals in intermediate drafts.

## FAQ

**camelCase and snake_case?** Use those modes when shipping to code or data files; do not use them for marketing H1s.

**Languages other than English?** Capitalization rules differ; review with a native speaker for public UI.

**Email subject lines?** Convert, then check mobile truncation by eye.

## CMS and design handoff

Designers often retype headlines and reintroduce chaos. Deliver a locked list after case conversion. If the CMS has a "title" field separate from "body," convert only the title field batch.

## Accessibility note

All caps in long strings hurts readability and screen-reader experience when abused. Prefer title case or sentence case for sentences. Reserve upper case for short labels.

## Batching without burnout

Do not convert the whole company wiki in one night. Convert the next launch surface: landing page, email 1, and PDF one-pager. Consistency on customer-facing surfaces beats perfection in every archive doc.

## Style guide one-pager

Write five lines: H1 rule, H2 rule, button rule, email subject rule, ad headline rule. Post it where writers and designers both see it. Case Converter executes the rule; the one-pager decides the rule.

## Acronyms and product versions

API, EU, and v2 need human review after automatic title case. Build a personal do-not-mangle list for your brand. Revisit the list when you launch a product with odd capitalization.

## Multilingual UI

If you ship Spanish or German UI strings, do not assume English title case maps cleanly. Convert carefully and have a native reviewer check public navigation labels.

## Diff discipline for docs sites

When you bulk-convert headings in a docs repo, review the diff. Automatic case changes can touch code-like identifiers inside headings if authors put them there. Revert those lines.

## Pairing with design tokens

Some design systems encode text transform in CSS (uppercase buttons). If CSS already forces upper case, converting the source to upper case can double-encode intent and confuse translators. Prefer source in natural case when CSS transforms at render time—unless your CMS strips CSS on export.

## Seasonal campaign freezes

Before a big launch, freeze headline case for 48 hours. Last-minute uppercasing by a partner reintroduces chaos. Convert once, lock the sheet, and require change requests for any title edit.

## Internal vs external surfaces

Internal wikis can be messier. External marketing pages should not. Spend case-conversion time where customers look first: homepage, pricing, and checkout microcopy.

## Checklist before you publish a page

Headings match house case. Buttons match house case. No accidental all-caps paragraphs. Brand names spelled as the brand spells them. Slug regenerated if the title words changed. That five-line check prevents most public embarrassment.
