## What belongs on every invoice

Your business identity, client identity, invoice number, issue date, due date, line items with quantities and rates, tax if applicable, total, and payment instructions. Missing any of these creates back-and-forth. Optional: PO number, project code, late fee note if your terms include one.

## Numbering that survives audits

Use a monotonic scheme: INV-2026-0112 or client code plus sequence. Never reuse numbers. Store a simple log: number, client, amount, date sent, date paid. When a client asks for a copy in March, you can find INV-2026-0112 without scrolling chat.

## Worked example: freelance design deposit

Two lines: discovery workshop $900, UI deposit $1,500, total $2,400, due in 7 days, invoice INV-2026-118. Client pays from the PDF without asking what "deposit" covered because the line text already said it. You log paid on the due week and issue a final invoice later for the remainder.

## Taxes and honesty

If you charge tax, show it as its own line or a clear total breakdown. If you do not charge tax, do not invent a tax line. Wrong tax display creates accounting pain for both sides. This guide is operational, not tax advice—confirm with your adviser for your jurisdiction.

## Common mistakes

Reusing the same invoice number after a correction. Changing scope without a new line or a credit note process. Sending PNG screenshots of invoices instead of PDF. Burying payment details in a logo-heavy header nobody reads. Forgetting to update the due date when you re-issue.

## FAQ

**Credit notes?** Issue a clear negative or adjustment document with a new number; do not silently edit history.

**Partial payments?** Record them in your log and state remaining balance on the next communication.

**Multi-currency?** State currency explicitly on every total line.

## Payment instructions that work

Name the method: bank transfer, card link, or check. Include only details you are willing to send by email. If bank details change, re-issue a new invoice version rather than relying on an old PDF in the client's AP queue.

## Design restraint

Readable beats ornate. High-contrast text, clear totals, and enough margin to print. Fancy backgrounds that obscure numbers create "I can't read the total" tickets.

## Mini case: the missing PO

A corporate client needs a PO on every invoice. Your first PDF omitted it and sat in AP for twelve days. You re-issued INV-2026-118b with PO-4419 in the field and payment arrived. Add PO as a required field in your pre-send checklist when the client is enterprise.

## Corrections workflow

If you typo a rate, void or credit the old number per your process and send a new invoice number. Do not ask the client to "ignore the last PDF" without a paper trail. AP departments need clean numbers.

## Pre-send checklist (print it)

Invoice number unique. Client legal name correct. PO present if required. Line descriptions match the proposal. Rates match the SOW. Tax treatment correct. Due date in the future. Payment details current. PDF opens. Total readable on mobile. Then send.

## Time tracking to lines

If you bill hourly, export hours from your tracker and turn each project phase into a line. Do not invent hours on the invoice screen under deadline pressure. The generator formats; your time log is the source of truth.

## Deposits and milestones

State milestone names that match the contract. "Milestone 2 — development complete" is easier for AP than "phase 2." Attach or reference the SOW page if the client asks.

## Late invoices and relationships

A correct invoice sent late still beats a perfect invoice never sent. Block a weekly billing hour on the calendar. Tools remove formatting friction; they do not create the habit.

## Recordkeeping

Save the sent PDF, the cover email, and the payment confirmation in one matter folder. When tax season or a dispute arrives, that folder is the difference between calm and chaos.

## Multi-entity freelancers

If you bill under two legal names, never mix letterheads. Separate invoice number sequences per entity. The generator will print whatever identity you enter—accuracy is on you.

## Attachments

When a client needs timesheets or screenshots with the invoice, list them in the cover email and keep the invoice PDF itself clean. A 20 MB attachment pile is a different problem from a clear one-page bill—compress supporting PDFs separately if needed.
