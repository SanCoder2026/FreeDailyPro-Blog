## Estimate, then platform template

Defaults are starting values, not guarantees. Platforms differ and change paper and bleed specs. Treat the result as an estimate and **compare with the cover template the platform generates** for your exact trim, page count, and paper before final art. KDP, for example, publishes its own generator and spine guidance—download that template and compare fold lines.

Not for hardcover: hardcover adds board, wrap, and hinge allowances—use the printer’s hardcover template.

5. Read spine width (page count × thickness per page + any allowance you enter). One sheet is two pages.
6. Note full wrap width (back + spine + front + bleed) and height with bleed.
7. Use the diagram: spine, fold lines, optional 0.25 in inner margin guide, optional barcode box on back.

## Odd page counts

Printed books are built from sheets with a page on each side, so counts are normally even. Using the final even count gives the correct spine. If the tool warns about odd counts, fix pagination (blank pages) rather than ignoring the warning.

## Barcode box

If you show a barcode box on the back (example 2×1.2 in, a quarter inch from trim in the tool’s guide), leave that real estate empty of critical design. Platforms may also place barcodes—follow their current rules.

## Common mistakes

- Designing on an old page count after adding a chapter.
- Using hardcover mental math for paperback wrap.
- Trusting only the estimate and skipping the platform template.
- Editing thickness randomly without platform documentation.
- Placing text across the spine fold without checking spine width at final count.

Jordan sends the designer: trim, page count, paper, platform, spine inches, full wrap size, and a link to the platform template. That single note prevents “why doesn’t the barcode fit” emails.

## Paper type changes mid-project

Switching cream to white or standard color to premium changes thickness and spine. Re-run the calculator the same day you change paper in the print setup. Designers hate learning the spine grew after they locked the barcode.

## Dual platform releases

If you print on both KDP and Ingram, calculate twice. Do not assume one spine width fits both. Keep two cover masters named by platform.

## Proof copy still required

Even perfect math benefits from a proof copy. Hold the book and check whether spine text is comfortable. Math gets you to print; the proof gets you to “ship.”


## Bleed and safety

Bleed is not decoration—it is insurance against trim variance. Keep critical text inside safety margins the platform documents. Spine text needs both width accuracy and vertical centering comfort once the book is in hand.

## FAQ

**How is spine calculated?** Page count × page thickness (+ allowance).
