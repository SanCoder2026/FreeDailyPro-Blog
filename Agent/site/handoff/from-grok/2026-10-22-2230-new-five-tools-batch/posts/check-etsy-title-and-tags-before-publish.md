## What it checks (and what it does not)

**Does:** character and count limits, repeated tags (even with different capitalization), title words not used in any tag, symbols Etsy usually rejects in tags.

**Does not:** tell you which words will rank or what buyers search. It has no search data. Word choice stays yours.

## Worked example: ceramic mug listing

- Title draft under 140 characters, product + material + use case
- Tags: 13 lines, each ≤20 characters, no duplicates
- Coverage: important title words appear somewhere in tags when useful
- Done definition: checker shows no limit violations and no accidental repeats

## Steps on Etsy Title and Tag Checker

1. Write the title offline with the real product facts only.
2. Open [Etsy Title and Tag Checker](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/etsy-listing-checker).
3. Paste the title; fix length if over 140.
4. Paste tags separated by commas, semicolons, or one per line (extra spaces stripped).
5. Clear repeats the tool finds—even when capital letters differ.
6. Review tags with symbols Etsy usually rejects; rewrite them.
7. Note title words none of your tags use; add a tag only if it is a real search-worthy phrase you want.
8. Copy the cleaned title and tags back into Etsy.
9. Save a plain-text backup of the final set for seasonal refreshes.
10. Recheck after any last-minute “creative” edit in the Etsy form.

## Coverage without keyword stuffing

When the tool lists title words missing from tags, that is a **gap report**, not a mandate to force every word into a tag. Prioritize buyer language. Do not invent thirteen near-identical variants that waste the 13-tag budget.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming the checker is a ranking oracle.
- Using all 13 tags as synonyms of one phrase.
- Special characters and emoji in tags that trigger reject hints.
- Pasting a title with trailing spaces that push character count.
- Publishing from memory after the check without pasting the cleaned text.

## Seasonal refresh habit

Avery keeps a spreadsheet: listing ID, title, 13 tags, last check date. When spring colors change, she re-runs the checker instead of guessing lengths in the phone app.

## Multi-listing batch day

On batch day, check every title/tag set before bulk scheduling photos. Limit failures at 10 p.m. are worse than catching them at noon.

## Mobile listing traps

Editing titles on the phone often truncates what you think you saved. After any mobile edit, paste back into the checker. Avery lost a full day of ads once because a title silently exceeded length after an app “suggestion.”

## Tag research still happens elsewhere

Use your own sales history, Etsy search suggestions, and competitor titles for word choice. Run those candidates through the checker so they fit limits. The two steps stay separate on purpose.

## Variation listings

If you clone a listing for a new colorway, re-check tags. Copied sets often keep color names that no longer match, wasting tags and confusing buyers.


## Character counting edge cases

Some characters and emoji count differently than you expect. If the checker and the Etsy form disagree by one or two characters, shorten the title until both are happy. Never ship at the exact maximum if the form still warns.

## Archive of winners

When a listing converts well, archive its exact title and tag set. Future seasonal variants start from a limit-clean winner instead of a blank box.
