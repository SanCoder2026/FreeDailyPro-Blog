[Blind CV Redactor](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/blind-cv-redactor) removes name, contact details, address, birth date, gender words, and similar details from resume **text** for an unbiased first review—processing stays in the browser.

This guide covers one job only: produce a readable anonymized text version for screening, then human-check it.

## Text only, not a full layout PDF editor

It works on text (paste or PDF/text with real text—not a scan). Photos, logos, and layout are not carried over as a designed PDF. ## Worked example: first-round panel pack

- Inputs: 12 resumes as text or text-based PDF
- Remove: candidate name parts, email/phone, address, photo/marital/nationality lines, gender words; optional years if policy says so
- Extra terms: schools, hometowns, nicknames, old surnames
- Done definition: Rina cannot identify the person on a cold read; skills and scope remain clear

## Steps on Blind CV Redactor

1. Collect text-based resumes (OCR scans first if needed elsewhere).
2. Open [Blind CV Redactor](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/blind-cv-redactor).
3. Paste text or drop a PDF/text file (over ~20 MB may be slow).
4. Enter candidate name parts to remove.
5. Add extra terms (school, hometown, clubs, prior surnames) one per line.
6. Toggle photo/marital/nationality lines, gender words, years as your policy requires.
7. Optionally show markers like `[EMAIL]` for what was removed.
8. Read the output as if you never met the person—anything identifying goes back into extra terms.
9. Export/share only the redacted text with the panel.
10. Keep the original sealed until later rounds per your process.

## It will not catch everything

Common formats are found; edge nicknames and rare clubs need extra terms. Always read once. It does not check legal compliance and does not decide who to hire.

## Common mistakes

- Sharing the redacted file in a thread that still has the original name in the email subject.
- Leaving graduation years when the panel is sensitive to age proxies—decide policy first.
- Expecting scanned image PDFs to work without text.
- Treating redaction as a complete bias solution (structured scorecards still matter).
- Uploading resumes to random third-party “AI screeners” against policy—this tool stays local to the tab.

## Limits and good to know

## Panel instructions

Tell reviewers: score only skills and scope; do not try to re-identify candidates; report if identity leaks through. That instruction is as important as the tool.

## When to reveal identity

Define the stage: after skills score ≥ X, coordinator unblinds. Do not unblind in the same email thread as the redacted pack. Separate channels reduce accidental leaks.

## Multi-language resumes

Name patterns differ by culture. Extra terms lists should include transliterations. When unsure, have a second reviewer try to identify the candidate from the redacted text.

## Data retention

Delete redacted and original copies from shared drives per your retention policy after the role closes. Browser-side processing does not remove the files you downloaded.

## Portfolio links

URLs often contain names (`jane-doe-portfolio.com`). Extra-term the domain or replace with `[PORTFOLIO]` before sharing. Same for LinkedIn URLs.

## Internal vs agency recruiting

If an agency already knows identity, still redact for the client panel. Document who holds the key file. Dual copies without a keyholder create chaos.

## Structured skills excerpt

Some teams paste only the skills and experience sections into the redactor and drop the header entirely. That can be cleaner than fighting every header field—as long as you still catch identity in bullets (e.g., “Founder of Doe LLC”).

## Training the panel

Ten minutes of training on the scorecard beats sophisticated redaction alone. Blind text plus biased scoring questions still fails the goal.


## Email and filename hygiene

Save redacted files as `Role_CandidateA_redacted.txt` without the person’s name in the filename. Filenames leak identity as often as headers do.

## Re-run after candidate updates

If the candidate sends a revised resume mid-process, re-redact from scratch. Partial hand-edits leave old phone numbers in forgotten footers.


## Audit sample

Spot-check two random redacted files every hiring round. If a reviewer can name the candidate, improve the extra-terms list before the full panel receives the pack.
