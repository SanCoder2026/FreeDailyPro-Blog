## Not a fire or access authority

Leave aisles roughly 2–3 metres (about 8–10 feet) wide and **check local fire and access rules**, which this planner does not know. Red outlines mark booths that overlap or stick out of the hall. ## Common mistakes

- Fitting booths first and shrinking aisles later.
- Treating the tool as fire-code approval.
- Closing the tab without exports.
- Planning multi-level or curved rooms the tool cannot model.
- Rotating booths (rectangles only, no rotation).

## Limits and good to know

- Plan drawn in the browser; nothing uploaded or kept.
- Rectangles only, one hall per plan; PDF is A4.

## Sales use of CSV

Booth number, size, and coordinates in CSV help price tiers and avoid double-selling the same space.

## Stage and demo zones

Place stages where they do not block emergency routes or main aisles. Even though the tool is not a fire certifier, bad stage placement is obvious in a walkthrough PDF.

## Exhibitor preview packs

Email each exhibitor their booth number callout on the PNG plus nearest aisle. Reduces “where am I?” messages on build day.


## Traffic flow story

Number booths so the natural walking path from entrance to café to exit makes sense. Random numbering forces exhibitors to give “we’re opposite the red banner” directions.

## Anchor exhibitors

Place large anchors where they draw traffic without blocking exits. Even a sketch tool makes bad anchor placement obvious when you export the PNG and squint.

## CSV to contract

When a booth is sold, lock its number in the CSV and mark it sold in exhibitor notes. Re-export the plan so operations and sales share one map version.

## Build-day paper

Print two A4 plans: one for ops, one taped at the loading dock. Phones die; paper still works in concrete halls.

## Power and water marks

If the venue map shows power drops, sketch those as notes on booths even if the tool is shapes-only. Exhibitors with demos care more about power than perfect rectangle art.

## Version control

`HallA_v7_sold80pct.pdf` in the filename stops ops from hanging v3 on the wall after sales sold four more booths.

## Dead corners

Corners far from entrances sell slower. Mark them clearly on the plan and price them honestly rather than surprising buyers on install day. The CSV exhibitor note field is a good place to store tier labels.

## Load-in arrows

Draw a simple load-in path on a printed plan with a pen if the digital tool cannot show arrows. Dock crews follow paper marks better than verbal “go left at the pillar” instructions.

## Indoor market variant

Farmers markets and craft fairs use the same rectangle logic at smaller scale. Keep aisle targets honest for strollers and carts, then export PNG for the public map board.

## Rain contingency

If outdoor booths move under a tent, redraw once and label the file outdoor-v-rain so volunteers do not build the sun plan in a storm.
