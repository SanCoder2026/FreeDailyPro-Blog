## Capture while the paper is still warm

Paste receipt text or read a photo in the browser. The tool finds vendor, date, total and currency and adds an expense you can correct. Receipts are not uploaded to FreeDailyPro servers for storage; reading happens in the browser. Photo reading depends on picture quality—always check the parsed amount before you trust a line. Expenses sit in the same project the budget planner uses, so spending appears against each budget line instead of in a disconnected notebook.

## Steps on Expense Capture Sheet

1. Open [Expense Capture Sheet](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/expense-capture-sheet).
2. Open or create the event project that already holds the budget lines.
3. Paste receipt text or photograph a paper receipt as the page allows.
4. Correct vendor, date, total and currency if the parse missed anything.
5. Assign the expense to the matching budget line.
6. Repeat for each receipt the same day it is spent.
7. Review the profit statement: income, costs by category, tax lines, result.
8. Save the project file to your device before you leave the venue Wi-Fi.
9. Export or copy the summary for the client or finance owner.
10. Archive the project file with the final invoices and bank exports.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until Monday to photograph a stack of faded thermal receipts.
- Leaving every expense in an “uncategorised” bucket so budget variance is meaningless.
- Trusting a blurry photo total without reading the printed figure.
- Treating the profit statement as filed tax accounts—it is planning information, not accounting software.
- Sharing a project file that also holds pass keys or door secrets with people who only need the expense summary.

## Edge cases

If two staff capture the same receipt, mark one as the owner of the project file to avoid double counting. If a vendor invoices later for a deposit already captured, reconcile by editing the expense rather than adding a second full amount. Multi-currency events need one reporting currency on the statement—convert carefully and note the rate used in the project notes.

## Communication template

When you send the profit summary, write: event dates, reporting currency, which receipts are still outstanding, and whether tax lines are estimates or confirmed. That paragraph saves a finance call.

## Same-day capture beats weekend archaeology

Thermal receipts fade. Card alerts bury themselves in banking apps. The only reliable habit is a five-minute capture block at lunch and another after the last vendor leaves. Put the phone camera on the receipt while the ink is dark, confirm the parsed total aloud, and only then file the paper in a day envelope. If the photo is unreadable, type the total manually—the tool allows correction—and mark the line “hand entered” in the vendor field so finance knows why no image exists.

Teams that wait until Monday routinely lose 3–8% of small spends: tips, parking, coffee services, tape runs. Those lines are exactly what push an event from “on budget” to “explain this variance.” Capture is cheaper than the post-mortem meeting.

## Client-billable versus internal costs

Tag or name expenses so billable lines are obvious when you build the client invoice later. Soft costs like staff meals may be internal only. The profit statement should match the story you will tell the client: which costs they fund, which you absorb.
