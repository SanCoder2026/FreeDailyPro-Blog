A unique PO number on the order, the invoice and the delivery note makes three-way matching faster. Tax percentage with a label and shipping amount both add to the total. ## Common mistakes

- Reusing PO numbers until two invoices claim the same id.
- Omitting delivery address when bill-to and ship-to differ.
- Forgetting tax or shipping that the supplier will add anyway.
- Treating the PDF signature line as a full contract for high-risk buys.
- Changing prices on email only without revising the PO.

## Edge cases

Standing orders: issue a new PO number per release or clearly mark call-off references. Blanket POs: state maximum value in notes. Multi-currency: keep one currency per PO.

## Matching checklist for finance

PO number present, quantities ≤ ordered, unit prices match, tax rate matches, shipping matches or is explained, delivery location correct.

## Numbering scheme that scales

Use a year prefix and a monotonic sequence (PO-2026-0001). Do not reset sequences per supplier. Store the PDF under that number and require suppliers to echo it on invoices and delivery notes. When finance pays, the PO number should appear on the payment memo. That single habit collapses month-end matching time.

## Exceptions and rush orders

Rush buys still deserve a PO, even if the sequence number is assigned after a phone call. Write “confirming verbal order of DATE” in the notes so audit trails stay complete. If a supplier cannot meet a line, issue a change PO or a written cancellation rather than leaving the original PDF as if it were still live. Close completed POs in your tracker so open orders do not haunt month-end.

## Receiving against the PO

When goods arrive, mark quantities received on a printed PO copy or in your tracker before finance sees the invoice. Short-ships should block full payment until a credit or revised invoice arrives. That receiving step is where PO value compounds.

## Change orders without chaos

When quantity or price changes after the original PO, issue a revised PDF with a suffix (PO-2026-118-A) and state what changed in the notes. Suppliers should reference the latest revision on invoices. Finance should reject invoices that cite cancelled PO numbers. A simple shared tracker with status Open / Partial / Closed prevents double ordering from two managers.

## Blanket and standing POs

For recurring weekly produce or office supplies, a standing PO can define max monthly value and unit prices, with each delivery referencing the same number plus a delivery date. Still generate a confirming PDF when prices change mid-year.

Attach the PO PDF to the same email thread as the supplier quote when possible, and restate the quote reference in the PO notes. That link prevents “we quoted different lead times” disputes. For international suppliers, include Incoterms in the notes if your company uses them.

Train new buyers to never place a stock order in chat without a PO number, even for small amounts—small amounts add up in unmanaged spend.
