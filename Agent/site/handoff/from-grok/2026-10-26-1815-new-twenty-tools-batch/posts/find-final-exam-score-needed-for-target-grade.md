## Weights, not letter grades, drive the math

Enter current grade as a percentage, not a letter. If the instructor gave points, divide by total available and multiply by 100. Final weight is the percent the exam counts (30 means 30%). The tool solves for the final score that produces your target course grade. If the needed score is above 100%, that target cannot be reached with the final alone—ask about extra credit or re-sits, or aim one grade lower. “Secured” means your current grade is high enough that even 0 on the final still yields that letter. Grade cut-offs differ by school; always check the syllabus for the exact percentage an A or B needs.

## Common mistakes

- Entering a letter grade into a field that expects a percentage.
- Using the wrong final weight (for example counting midterms twice).
- Ignoring school-specific cut-offs and assuming 90 is always an A.
- Treating a result over 100% as a calculator bug instead of an unreachable target.
- Forgetting to re-run after a dropped quiz changes the current average.

## Edge cases

Multiple finals or lab practicals: compute a combined remaining weight or run the tool once per remaining assessment with adjusted current grade. Pass/fail courses: set the target to the pass threshold from the syllabus. Incomplete grades: wait until the current percentage is final before trusting “Secured” labels.

## Study planning from the number

If you need 88% on the final, break the exam outline into topics and assign hours; if you need 102%, switch the target to the next realistic grade and protect sleep. Pair the number with a written outline rather than rereading the whole textbook the night before.

## Turn the number into a study calendar

Once you know you need, say, 86% on the final, reverse-plan the days you still have. List major topics from the syllabus, star the ones that historically carry the most points, and assign blocks of time that fit around work or labs. Recalculate after any makeup grade posts, because a two-point rise in the current average can drop the required final score by more than two points when the final is heavily weighted.

If the tool says a grade is Secured, do not coast into a zero—many curves and participation policies still expect a serious attempt. Use the spare mental room to strengthen weaker courses instead of gambling the secured grade away.

## Weighted categories beyond a single final

If your course uses labs 20%, midterms 30%, final 50%, compute the current percentage from all completed work before you enter it. Some students accidentally enter only the midterm average and then panic at an impossible final target. When the syllabus offers optional final replacement rules, run the calculator twice: once with the rule, once without, and keep the syllabus PDF beside your plan.

## Talking to instructors

If the required score exceeds 100%, bring the calculator result to office hours as a planning question, not a demand. Instructors can clarify extra credit, curves or exam formats more usefully when you show the arithmetic.
