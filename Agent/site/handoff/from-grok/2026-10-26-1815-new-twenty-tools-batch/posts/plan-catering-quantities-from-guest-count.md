## Planning amounts are a starting point, not a contract

Seated dinners often plan one main course per person; standing receptions may plan roughly six canapés in the first hour. Water, soft drinks and coffee usually scale with everyone and with extra hours. Alcohol is counted only for the share of guests you say drink it, with a bottle estimate (for example five glasses per bottle of wine). The extra percentage adds margin for seconds, late arrivals and spills, then rounds up. Set margin to 0 if the caterer already pads orders. Special meals from your guest list—vegetarian, halal, gluten-open and similar—should be ordered separately from the general count and listed clearly for the kitchen.

## Common mistakes

- Ordering general mains without subtracting special-diet plates that were already counted in the head count.
- Leaving margin at a high default when the caterer already adds 15%, so trays double-pad.
- Ignoring hours so soft drinks run out in hour three of a four-hour open house.
- Treating the bottle estimate as a legal bar inventory count rather than a planning aid.
- Editing the CSV by hand until it no longer matches the project, then arguing with the kitchen from two different lists.

## Edge cases worth planning

If RSVPs still swing by 20% the week before, run two exports (low and high) and label them by date. If outdoor heat will spike water use, raise the water line manually rather than hoping margin covers it. If two planners edit different project files, appoint one owner so the caterer receives a single CSV.

## Hand-off note to the caterer

When you send the list, write: event date, head count used, margin percent, which lines you changed from defaults, and how many special meals by type. That single paragraph prevents “which version is final” threads the morning of the event.

## Children and vendors on site

If children eat smaller portions or vendors are not fed from the guest count, adjust head count inputs rather than hoping margin covers the difference. Write the assumption in the CSV filename or a cover note: “120 adults, 15 children counted as 0.5, 8 vendors separate.” Caterers make better quotes from explicit assumptions than from silent optimism.
