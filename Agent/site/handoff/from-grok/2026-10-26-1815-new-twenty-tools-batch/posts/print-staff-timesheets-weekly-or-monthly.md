## What the PDF is—and is not

Type one name per line (or take names from a guest list when linked), pick the first date and period, choose whether to pre-fill a standard day, preview, and download. Overnight spans across midnight count correctly (22:00 to 06:00 is 8 hours). Pay, if a rate is entered, is hours times one rate—no overtime rules, tax or holiday logic. Labels are in English. Not payroll software and not a legal archive by itself. Browser-built; nothing uploaded; project erased on tab close unless you save a project file.

## Worked example: three-day festival crew

Ops needs timesheets for 22 stewards on a three-day festival. Names pasted, period set to cover the festival week, standard shift pre-filled as 10:00–18:00 with a break note in instructions. PDF prints 22 pages. Stewards amend actual times in pen, sign daily, and return sheets to the ops desk before departure. A spare blank page covers walk-in helpers.

## Common mistakes

- Treating hours×rate as net pay after tax.
- Letting sheets leave without signatures.
- Using one period for people on totally different shift patterns without blank overrides.
- Forgetting spare blanks for day-of volunteers.
- Assuming the tool stores historical timesheets after the tab closes.

## Edge cases

Union overtime: use blank sheets and compute pay in payroll systems. Multi-site crews: generate separate PDFs per site date range. Minors/volunteers: follow local labour rules outside the tool.

## Manager script at handback

“Write actual start and end, initial corrections, sign the day line, return the sheet before you leave.” Three sentences prevent most disputes.

## From paper back into payroll

Treat signed sheets as source documents: scan to PDF, name with dates, and enter hours into payroll software separately. The generator’s pay estimate is a cross-check, not a payslip. If a steward alters pre-filled times, require a supervisor initial beside the change. Store sheets for the retention period your region requires for wage records.

## Rain and outdoor crews

Carry a clipboard with waterproof sleeves for outdoor festivals. Wet paper that cannot be read later is the same as lost hours. Photograph each signed sheet before leaving site as a backup scan.

## Breaks and unpaid time

If local rules require break notation, instruct staff to write break start/end in the notes column even when the grid is simple. The PDF is a capture form; your policy fills the gaps. For volunteers, leave the rate blank and treat the sheet as attendance proof for grants or insurance logs.

## Multi-week festivals

Generate separate PDFs per week rather than one giant monthly sheet that confuses day labels. Name files festival-week1-stewards.pdf and store them with the signed scans in the same folder tree.

When using pre-fill, tell staff that pre-filled times are a starting point, not a claim. Alterations must be visible. Managers should initial changes the same day. For contractors billing clients, attach the signed sheet scans to the client invoice packet so hours and bills align.

If two rates apply (weekday vs night), leave rate blank on the sheet and compute pay offline—the simple hours times one rate model is intentionally limited. Note the policy on the print pack cover page.

## Lost sheet protocol

If a sheet disappears, reissue a blank for that person with a note “reissue for DATE range” and require manager sign-off that prior hours were reconstructed from door logs or messages. Do not invent hours. The printable PDF is easy to replace; the integrity of the hours is not.
