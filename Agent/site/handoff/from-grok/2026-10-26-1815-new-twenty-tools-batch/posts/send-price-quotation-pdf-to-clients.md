## Quote vs invoice

A quote is an offer with a validity window, not a request for payment. When the client accepts, issue an invoice that mirrors the lines. Discounts and tax (VAT, GST, sales tax labels) apply in the totals. Currency list formats regional symbols. The optional signature line is for paper or PDF signing—there is no online accept button inside the tool. Built in the browser; nothing typed is uploaded or stored by FreeDailyPro.

## Common mistakes

- Omitting a valid-until date so prices float forever.
- Hiding exclusions until after acceptance.
- Quoting without tax clarity in regions that require it on offers.
- Changing scope on chat without revising the quote number.
- Using a quote as an invoice and confusing accounts payable.

## Edge cases

Retainer quotes: state hours included and overage rates. Goods plus services: separate lines for clarity. Multi-option quotes: issue Quote A/B PDFs rather than one ambiguous document.

## Follow-up cadence

Send, remind at halfway to expiry, final reminder 48 hours before valid-until, then archive as expired if silent.

## Scope boundaries worth writing every time

List deliverables as lines, then add a short exclusions paragraph: rounds of revision, stock photography budget, rush fees, travel, third-party licences. Clients rarely object to clear exclusions on day one; they object to surprise invoices on day thirty. When acceptance arrives after the valid-until date, issue a revised quote with a new number rather than silently honouring old prices if costs moved.

## Deposits and payment terms on the quote

State deposit percent, due date, and accepted payment methods in the notes so acceptance is not only about scope. When a client asks for a “rough ballpark” in chat, still follow with a dated PDF if the number might be relied on. Archive declined quotes; patterns in lost deals teach pricing better than memory.

## Quote numbers people can cite

Use Q-YYYY-### and put that number in the email subject. When a client accepts “the quote you sent,” you both know which PDF. Reuse of untitled filenames is how scope arguments begin.

## Optional packages on separate quotes

Good / better / best packages should be three clearly numbered quotes or three clearly separated sections with three totals—not one ambiguous lump. Clients who “accept the middle option” need a PDF that still makes sense six weeks later when accounts payable asks what was included.

## Currency and tax clarity

If you quote internationally, state currency explicitly near the total and whether tax is included. Misunderstandings here create unpaid invoices more often than scope disputes.

For retainers, quote monthly hours and the rate for overage on separate lines. For fixed-bid projects, list milestones as lines with amounts that sum to the total. Ambiguous single-line quotes (“website project”) create endless change requests.

When a quote expires, send a short note that it lapsed and offer a refreshed PDF if the client returns. That habit protects you when supplier costs moved during the gap.

## From accepted quote to project kickoff

On acceptance, store the signed or explicitly accepted PDF next to the project brief and open the first invoice for the deposit the same day. Link the quote number in the invoice notes. Kickoff without a filed quote is how unpaid discovery work multiplies.
