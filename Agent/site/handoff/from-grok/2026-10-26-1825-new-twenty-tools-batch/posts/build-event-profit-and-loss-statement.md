## What the tool does

Turn your event income and costs into a profit and loss statement with planned versus actual, tax lines and per-head figures. Numbers come from income and cost entries in your project (same ones used with expense, invoice, and related flows). Amounts shown with tax included; statement also shows tax inside income and costs using rates you entered. Per-head figures divide income, cost, and profit by heads—default confirmed and attended guests with plus-ones. Only project-currency entries count; others are reported as left out. ## Steps

1. Ensure income and cost entries exist in the project.
2. Open Event Profit Ledger.
3. Confirm project currency and tax rates on entries.
4. Set or accept heads for per-head figures.
5. Review planned versus actual lines.
6. Note how many non-currency entries were excluded.
7. Export or screenshot the statement for the board pack.
8. Pair with narrative variances offline.
9. Save the project after final entries.
10. Do not treat the ledger as filed tax advice.

## Common mistakes

- Missing tax rates on taxed lines.
- Wrong headcount for per-head math.
- Ignoring excluded multi-currency rows.
- Calling the PDF tax advice.
- Reading profit before last invoices post.

## Heads definition

Default uses confirmed and attended with plus-ones. Override only with a written definition so finance and marketing argue less.

## Tax lines

If an entry is marked taxed with no rate, tax is not counted—the tool lists those gaps so you can fix them.

## Planned vs actual

Budget lines make the variance conversation possible. Enter plans early, not only actuals at the end.

## Sponsor income

Code sponsors clearly so they do not look like ticket sales in the P&L story.

## Board one-pager

Lead with total profit, per-head, and top three variances—then attach detail.

## Production log

For the build event profit and loss statement workflow, write one line of settings and export date beside the final download so a reprint does not rely on memory.

## Comp tickets

Comped seats are not zero-cost—they are marketing cost or sponsor fulfillment. Code them so ticket revenue is not silently inflated.

## Refund timing

Post refunds in the period they happen so the P&L month matches cash, or note accrual clearly for the board.

## Headcount policy memo

Write one sentence defining heads for per-head metrics and paste it under the exported statement. Marketing counting registrations while finance counts attended check-ins is a classic false fight.

Re-run the ledger after the last refund batch posts so leadership does not celebrate a number that will drop next week.
