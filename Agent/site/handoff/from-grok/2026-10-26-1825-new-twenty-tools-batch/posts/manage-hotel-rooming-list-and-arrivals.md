## Cut-off discipline

Put the hotel cut-off on the team calendar with an owner. Unused rooms after cut-off are often your cost.

## Roommate policy

State who can share and who cannot (executives, genders, accessibility) before import so the list does not thrash.

## Accessibility rooms

Flag access needs early—hotels have finite accessible inventory.

## Shuttle link

Arrivals without times still help day-level shuttle counts; put flight detail in notes when you have it.

## Privacy

Rooming lists contain personal data—share only with hotel and ops who need them.

## Production log

Write one line of settings and export date next to the final file for the manage hotel rooming list and arrivals job so a reprint does not start from zero.

## Late arrival codes

Tell the hotel who arrives after desk hours. Guests stuck outside at midnight become your emergency, not only the hotel’s.

## Master account rules

State what charges go to master versus personal cards. Ambiguity creates invoice fights two weeks later.

## Hotel liaison pack

Send the rooming list, block reference, cut-off date, and late-arrival list as one PDF pack. Hotels ignore fragmented emails. Confirm receipt with a named hotel contact, not a generic reservations inbox alone.

Room labels should use the name guests expect at the door—preferred names beat legal names when those differ on the guest list.

## Extra field practice

Share a privacy-minimized list with drivers (first name, arrival window) and a fuller list with the hotel (legal name, room type). Do not blast full lists to every vendor. When plus-ones share rooms, state who is the primary folio contact. After cut-off, send a final delta of releases and adds with a bright subject line so the hotel does not miss it in a long thread.


## Capacity and contingency

For manage hotel rooming list and arrivals, write a short contingency: if the primary laptop fails, which backup device opens the project; if the printer fails, which file is already on a USB stick; if the venue network fails, which steps still work offline. Contingencies written after the failure are just stories. Contingencies written before the event are operations.

Assign names, not roles only—roles change when people call in sick. List those people by name in the notes file that sits beside the manage hotel rooming list and arrivals export.
