## Steps

1. Ensure guests exist in the guest list or this tool’s table.
2. Set arrival and departure dates for each traveler.
3. Add flight or time notes for transport where known.
4. Read the board for busiest arrival and departure days.
5. Size shuttles and desk staffing to those peaks.
6. Export CSV for the transport vendor.
7. Update the board when flights change—same day.
8. Save the project with the guest list.
9. Brief welcome desk on peak hours, not only peak days.
10. After the event, compare planned vs actual for next year.

## Common mistakes

- Dates only in email, not on the guest list.
- Ignoring the busiest day metric.
- No flight notes when drivers need them.
- Frozen CSV while flights still change.
- Planning only arrivals and forgetting departures.

## Limits and good to know

Dates come from the guest list fields. Times/flights live in notes and CSV—not a live airline feed.

## Shared fields

Edit dates here or in Guest List Tracker; they share. Pick one habit so two people do not overwrite each other.

## Busiest day staffing

Add greeters for the peak arrival day even if average days look calm.

## Driver packs

CSV with notes beats a group chat of screenshots at 5 a.m.

## No-travel guests

Local guests without travel dates should not inflate shuttle counts—leave dates blank intentionally.

## Weather buffers

Peak arrival plus bad weather means longer desk lines—schedule breaks for staff.

## Production log

Write one line of settings and export date next to the final file for the plan guest arrivals and departures job so a reprint does not start from zero.

## Desk kit

Peak arrival days need printed boards, pens, and a charger strip. Digital-only desks fail when one tablet dies.

## Departure luggage

Departures need bag space and earlier wake-up shuttles. Plan departures with the same seriousness as red-carpet arrivals.

## Peak-hour staffing chart

Build a simple hour-by-hour greeter chart for the busiest arrival day. Average-day staffing on peak day is how lines form in the rain. Departures need earlier luggage staging than arrivals need welcome coffee.

Put a printed board at the desk even if the digital board is primary—power and Wi-Fi fail at the worst moment.

## Archive and naming for plan guest arrivals and departures

Store final exports under a folder named with the event or project code and the ISO date. Inside, keep `exports/`, `project/`, and `notes.txt`. In notes.txt for plan guest arrivals and departures, record approver name, device used on the day, and version label. That beats a desktop full of final2 copies.


## Extra field practice

Model shuttle seats with a 20 percent buffer on peak arrival hours. No-shows still leave you short if weather delays stack two flights into one window. For departures, publish bag-down times on the welcome sheet so guests do not all appear at the curb with thirty minutes left. Keep a runner dedicated to VIP arrivals so the main desk queue stays fair.


## Capacity and contingency

For plan guest arrivals and departures, write a short contingency: if the primary laptop fails, which backup device opens the project; if the printer fails, which file is already on a USB stick; if the venue network fails, which steps still work offline. Contingencies written after the failure are just stories. Contingencies written before the event are operations.

Assign names, not roles only—roles change when people call in sick. List those people by name in the notes file that sits beside the plan guest arrivals and departures export.
