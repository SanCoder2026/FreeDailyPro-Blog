## Payment chase loop

Each Friday, filter booked-not-paid and call the contact list. Update Paid the same day money hits the account so the board stays trustworthy for sales and ops.

## Floor plan alignment

Give each booth a number that matches your floor plan so booths line up between tracker and map. Misaligned numbering is the fastest way to an onsite argument at load-in.

## Contract packet

When status becomes Booked, attach the fee amount and booth number to the contract PDF you send offline. The tracker is the living ledger; the contract is the legal artifact.

## Refund and cancel path

Move Cancelled rows off live booth numbers the same day. Leave a note of refund amount in your offline finance file so the tool’s Paid field is not the only history.

## Sales handoff

Share a read-only export of available booths with sales each morning. If two reps sell the same number, the double-book warning is your last safety net—clear it before the floor map is public.

## Week-of-show freeze

Two days before load-in, freeze booth numbers. New sales go to a waitlist rather than reshuffling the floor under people already printing graphics for booth 18.

## Public map vs internal ledger

Publish a public map without unpaid internal notes. The tracker holds fee truth; the public PDF only shows company names and numbers you are willing to display.

## Sales and ops weekly ritual

Monday morning, export unpaid booked rows and assign chase owners by territory. Wednesday, re-check double-book warnings after any floor redraw. Friday, freeze numbers if the public map prints next week. That rhythm keeps the ledger honest without daily all-hands meetings.

When a company upgrades booth size, change the fee and booth fields in the same edit. Partial updates are how “Paid” still shows the old amount while the map shows a larger footprint.
