## Status words Sam actually uses

Sam limits ledger status to four values: draft, sent, paid, and write-off. He does not invent “almost paid” or “waiting on them.” If a client pays half, he marks a partial note in the expense or memo field and keeps status sent until the balance clears, then paid. Clear status makes the year-end CSV usable for his bookkeeper without a two-hour clean-up. When a job dies, write-off is honest; leaving it as sent forever inflates his open receivables and confuses next year’s planning.

## Weekly 20-minute close

Friday: open the desk, add any unsent ledger gaps, mark paid invoices, enter mileage for the week, save. Monthly: client packet for anyone who needs a bundle. Yearly: CSV for the bookkeeper.

## Mileage rate hygiene

Sam stores the rate he and his adviser agreed to use for the year in a note inside the project. When the IRS or local rules update, he changes the rate going forward and does not silently rewrite old trips. The desk totals miles times the rate you enter; it does not download official rates for you.

## Client packet contents

A client packet ZIP is not every file Sam ever made. It is the set that client should still have: latest proposal, signed summary, open invoice, and any receipt for expenses billed through. He excludes internal scratch PDFs. The packet is a professional hand-off, not a dump.

## Status hygiene on every send

Sam’s ledger rule is simple: the day a PDF leaves his outbox, a row exists with status and due date. “Sent” is temporary. When the client accepts a proposal, he marks accepted the same day. When an invoice is paid, he marks paid the same day. Leaving rows in “sent” for months is how December becomes archaeology.

Repeat clients get duplicated rows or recurring monthly flags so the next period starts with dates already sketched. He still reviews every new invoice number before it goes out; duplication is a starting point, not a blind copy of last month’s numbers.

## Expenses that belong with the client

Pass-through software, courier fees, and shipping on physical deliverables go on the client’s expense lines when they are billable. Internal tools he would buy anyway stay off the client packet.

## Bookkeeper hand-off without drama

The year-end CSV is only as good as eleven months of boring entries. Sam’s bookkeeper receives one flat file and a short note about mileage rate used. The desk does not convert currencies and does not decide deductibility. That boundary keeps Sam from pretending the desk is accounting software.

## Scenario: late invoice, clear ledger

A client pays invoice #1042 fourteen days late. Sam marks it paid on the day the transfer lands, leaves the original due date intact, and adds a short note on the row. He does not rewrite history to make the year look cleaner. When the bookkeeper asks about the gap, the row tells the story.

## When not to use Freelancer Desk

If you need automated payment collection, multi-user accounting, or tax filing, use proper accounting software. Freelancer Desk is for the solo operator who needs a client list, a send ledger, expenses, mileage, packets, and a year-end export without uploading client files to a vendor.

## Proposal to invoice without losing the thread

When a proposal converts, Sam does not delete the proposal row. He adds the invoice row and links them in the notes field with the proposal id. That way a dispute six months later still shows what was proposed versus what was billed. The client packet for that engagement includes both the accepted proposal summary and the open or paid invoice.
