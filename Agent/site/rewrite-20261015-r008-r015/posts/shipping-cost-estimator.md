## Step-by-step workflow

1. Pack one real unit the way you will ship it, including inserts and filler.
2. Weigh the packed parcel on a scale; write the number down.
3. Measure length, width, and height if dimensional weight may apply.
4. Open [Shipping Cost Estimator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/shipping-cost-estimator) and enter the values the form requests.
5. Convert ounces or grams with [Unit Converter](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/unit-converter) when your scale units differ from the form.
6. Record the estimate beside the SKU in a simple sheet.
7. Check how postage changes margin with [Percentage Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/percentage-calculator).
8. Adjust list price, shipping charge, or packaging, then re-estimate.

## Common mistakes

- Pricing from bare product weight instead of packed weight
- Ignoring dimensional weight on light bulky goods
- Using only local-zone rates when buyers are national
- Flat-rate shipping set below your expensive zones
- Forgetting return shipping on high-return categories
- Ordering custom mailers before testing one live packed shipment
- Mixing marketplace fee math with postage in one vague number

## Honest limits

Estimates depend on inputs and rate assumptions. Live print-time carrier quotes can differ. Confirm service levels on the carrier site before promising overnight or international delivery. This is general business information, not freight brokerage advice.


## Dimensional weight in plain language

Carriers may charge by volume when a box is large and light. Soft goods in oversized boxes get punished. Poly mailers often win for apparel; rigid kits may need boxes. Re-measure after every packaging change.

## Marketplace versus direct checkout

Platform fees stack on postage. A price that works on your own site can fail after marketplace fees plus shipping. Keep two columns: direct and marketplace.

## Weekly actual-versus-estimate review

Each week compare five orders’ actual postage to your sheet. Update SKUs that drift. If you always overestimate, you may be scaring buyers with high shipping — tune deliberately.

## Packaging experiments that pay back

Test one thinner mailer, one recycled insert, one smaller box. Re-weigh. Small grams matter at scale. Document the winning recipe in the SKU sheet.
