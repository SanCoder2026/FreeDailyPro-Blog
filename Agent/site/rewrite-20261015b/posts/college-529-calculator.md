## Inputs to gather first

Child age or years until enrollment, rough target balance, what you can save monthly now. State plan rules vary; read your plan documents for tax treatment.

## Numbered workflow

1. Write a target year and rough cost assumption from sources you trust for that year.
2. Open [College 529 Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/college-529-calculator).
3. Enter fields as labeled on the page.
4. Adjust monthly contribution until the plan looks plausible.
5. Model income changes with [Percentage Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/percentage-calculator).
6. If borrowing may fill a gap, sketch payments with [Loan Calculator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/loan-calculator).

## Revisit yearly

Inflation and school choices change. Re-run annually. Do not set and forget for a decade without a check.

## Common mistakes

- Treating calculator output as a guarantee of future returns.
- Ignoring plan fees and rules.
- Saving only in taxable cash without comparing plan benefits (depends on your situation).
- Hiding the plan from a co-parent who shares costs.

## Mini case

A family sets a modest monthly amount, re-checks when a raise hits 10%, uses percentage math to bump contributions, and keeps a loan sketch as a backup—not a first choice.

**Which state plan?** Research your state's benefits; the calculator does not replace plan selection.

**Multiple children?** Model separately.

## Choosing assumptions without fantasy

Use official college cost explorers and your own school shortlist rather than viral social posts. Note the academic year of any figure you cite. Inflation assumptions should be modest and revisited.

If grandparents contribute, write who owns the account and how gifts are documented. Family clarity prevents later conflict.

## Coordination with retirement

Do not fund college by silently raiding retirement without a plan. Run both goals as separate conversations. Loan Calculator sketches are for education gaps—not a reason to skip retirement matches if your situation values them.

## Tax and plan documents

Read your plan's program description. Tax treatment depends on jurisdiction and use of funds. Calculator output is not a filing position. For tax year specifics, use official revenue authority pages for the current year.

## Age-based vs static portfolios

Investment glide paths are plan features outside a contribution calculator. Know what you hold; rebalance policy is yours or your advisor's.

## Communication with students

As kids age, share the plan at a high level so major choices (private vs public) are informed. Numbers reduce surprise better than silence.

## Scholarship and aid uncertainty

Do not assume zero aid or full ride. Build a base savings plan that still works if aid is modest. Revisit when award letters arrive.

## Gap years and transfers

Timelines move. If enrollment slips a year, re-run contributions and withdrawal timing against plan rules. Early non-qualified withdrawals can have consequences—read plan and tax rules.

## Sibling spacing

Two children two years apart changes cash flow peaks. Model staggered enrollments rather than one blended blob number.
