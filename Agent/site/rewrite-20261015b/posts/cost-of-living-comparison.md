## Categories that move the needle

Housing, transit, food, childcare, and healthcare access often dominate. Entertainment is real but secondary. Write your actual lifestyle, not a generic tourist budget.

## Salary is not the whole story

Taxes, benefits, and commute time matter. A 15% raise with a 40% rent jump is not a win. Use Percentage Calculator to compare raise percent against rent percent change.

## Soft costs

Distance from family, climate, and dual-career constraints do not fit a calculator. Write them in a side column so they are not forgotten.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing only rent for a luxury building you will not rent.
- Ignoring commute costs.
- Using tourist restaurant prices as grocery reality.
- Forgetting deposits and moving fees in month one.

## Mini case

A designer compares mid-size city A to city B. Rent up 30%, transit down because of a walkable area. Percentage math on the offer shows a thin real gain. They negotiate remote hybrid days instead of accepting blindly.

## FAQ

**Are indexes perfect?** No. They are guides. Ground them with real listings.

**Family size?** Scale food and housing for your household.

**Short assignment?** Weight temporary housing differently than a permanent move.

## Building a personal index

Download five real rent listings in each city at the quality you would actually rent. Average them yourself. Plug those into your comparison mindset even if a tool uses broader indexes. Your lifestyle is not the average tourist row in a chart.

Track transit passes versus car ownership honestly. Insurance, parking, and wear matter if you keep a car.

## Offer negotiation angles

If the real cost gap is large, negotiate start-date flexibility, relocation stipend, or remote days. Bring a one-page cost summary—not a demand essay. Numbers open conversations better than vibes.

## Dual-income and family moves

Model childcare and second-commute costs. A raise for one partner can be erased by care costs. Include school quality as a non-dollar column.

## First-ninety-days cash

Deposits, furniture gaps, and double rent overlap destroy cash even when long-run math works. Keep a separate startup cash list outside the monthly steady-state comparison.

## Housing quality normalization

Comparing a luxury one-bedroom to a shared room is not analysis. Lock quality: same bedroom count, similar commute time band, similar safety notes from sources you trust. Then compare money.

Utilities sometimes hide in rent. Ask whether heat is included. Winter cities can surprise.

## Healthcare and benefits

Employer coverage differences can dwarf grocery spreads. List deductible and premium estimates as a separate line. Cost of living tools rarely capture your exact plan.

## Lifestyle anchors

If you train at a specialty gym or need a faith community, price those memberships. "Average" baskets ignore anchors that make you happy enough to stay.

## Currency and remote offers

Cross-border remote pay may be in a different currency. Convert carefully and consider tax residency rules with a professional when relevant. A calculator is not a tax opinion.
