## Measure like a carrier will

Weigh the packed product, not the empty craft. Include void fill and the box. Round up slightly so surprises are rare. If you only have grams and the form wants pounds, convert carefully with Unit Converter and write both numbers in your notes.

## Dimensions and dimensional weight

Long thin boxes can price by dimensional weight. Measure length, width, and height of the finished pack. If the live tool asks for dimensions, enter the packed size, not the product alone.

## Pricing decisions after the estimate

Either raise price, slim packaging, or offer paid shipping clearly. Free shipping is a marketing choice funded by margin—not a law of nature. Zone matters; a domestic average is not a global rate.

## Common mistakes

- Weighing the product without the box.
- Using wishful weight from a supplier page.
- Ignoring dimensional weight on light bulky items.
- Changing packaging after you locked the listing price.
- Forgetting marketplace programs that change who pays what.

## Mini case

## FAQ

**Is this a live carrier quote?** Treat it as planning math. Final labels may differ by service and address.

**International?** Duties and taxes differ; estimate postage only.

**Multiple SKUs?** Table pack weights per SKU family.

## Packaging experiments worth running

Try one thinner mailer versus a rigid box for non-fragile SKUs. Re-weigh and re-estimate. Sometimes a dollar of packaging saves three in postage—or the reverse. Write results in a simple sheet tied to SKU families.

For fragile goods, never under-box. Breakage refunds cost more than honest packaging weight. Include insert cards in the weighed sample if you always ship them.

International paperwork weight is real: customs forms in pouches add grams. Include them when you sell abroad.

## Carrier program awareness

Marketplace-labeled shipping can change the effective rate. If you use a platform program, compare its rate card to your self-ship estimate before you promise free shipping in the listing title. The estimator helps you think; the platform checkout may still rule.

Insurance and signature options add cost. Only enable them when product value or theft risk justifies it.

## Seasonal spikes

Holiday dimensional surcharges and peak fees appear without warning on some services. Re-run estimates in October if you sell seasonally. Old summer numbers lie.

## Warehouse reality versus kitchen-table packing

If you eventually hire pick-and-pack help, document the pack method with a photo and a weight range. Estimator math only works when warehouse staff pack like you did on day one. Drift in void fill changes postage silently.

Create a simple pack card per SKU family: box SKU, fill type, label placement, insert cards. Tape the card on the shelf. New helpers follow the card; postage stays predictable.

## Returns and dimensional surprises

Customers who return items in random boxes can reverse-logistics your margins. Estimate return-label cost for high-return categories. Some sellers price that risk into the product instead of "free returns" marketing.

## Multi-box orders

When an order splits into two boxes, estimate each. Combined checkout shipping promises can undercharge. Your system should know pack splits before the rate is shown to the buyer if you control checkout.

## Insurance math

Insure only when replacement cost and theft risk justify the fee. Write the rule: insure above amount X. Do not decide package-by-package in a rush at the post office.
