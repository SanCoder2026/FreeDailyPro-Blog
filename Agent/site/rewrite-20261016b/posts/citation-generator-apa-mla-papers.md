## Why citations fail at 2 a.m.

Paste from a random website page often misses DOI, retrieval date, or second authors. Generators format what you feed them. Messy input becomes messy output. Official PDFs and publisher pages beat random HTML mirrors. When both exist, prefer the version with a stable DOI.

## Numbered workflow

1. Read the syllabus for style (APA, MLA) and edition notes.
2. List every source you actually cite in the body.
3. Open [Citation Generator](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/citation-generator).
4. For each source, fill fields carefully: author order, year, title capitalization per style, container (journal name, site name), volume/issue/pages or URL/DOI.
5. Paste into your document with hanging indent settings your word processor supports.
6. Spot-check three entries against a handbook sample for website, journal article, and book chapter.
7. Run body length with [Word Counter](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/word-counter). Fix grammar with [Grammar Checker](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/grammar-checker) on the prose, not on the reference list punctuation the style guide owns.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing APA author-year in text with an MLA Works Cited layout.
- Leaving "n.d." when the page clearly shows a year in the footer.
- Truncating multi-author lists incorrectly for the chosen style.
- Linking citations to pages that moved; add access notes only when the style requires them.
- Polishing citations while the essay still exceeds the word cap—check [Word Counter](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/word-counter) early.

## What the tool does and does not do

The generator speeds consistent formatting. It does not replace reading the official style manual for edge cases (government docs, court cases, datasets). It does not invent missing authors. If a field is blank, leave it blank rather than inventing a name.

## FAQ

**Professor wants a different style?** Follow the syllabus, not a generator default.

**Can I cite a YouTube video?** Yes in most styles—use the fields for creator, upload date, and URL as the tool presents them, then verify against the handbook example for audiovisual works.

**In-text vs reference list?** Generators help the list. In-text citations still need author and year (APA) or author-page (MLA) matching that list exactly.

## Honest limits

Trial usage is 10 trial usages a day across FreeDailyPro tools. Large classes should still learn style rules for exams that ban web tools. Always re-open the PDF or page you cited before final lock.

## Style guide still wins

If the syllabus says APA 7, do not freestyle Chicago because a sample looked pretty. Generate, then skim every entry against the handbook example for that source type—website versus journal versus book chapter. Professors notice mismatched italics and wrong container titles faster than weak thesis statements sometimes.

## Fields people leave blank

Author order matters. Middle initials matter in APA more than students expect. Page ranges for book chapters are not optional when the chapter is what you read. For web pages, capture the date you accessed the material only when your style edition requires it. When a DOI exists, prefer it over a raw search URL that will rot.

## Group projects and shared libraries

One student should own the master reference list so formats stay consistent. Others paste their new sources into the same document after generating. Diff the list against the in-text citations before lock: every parenthesis in the body needs a matching entry, and every entry should be cited at least once unless the instructor wants a bibliography of consulted works.

## Edge cases worth a second look

Government PDFs, court opinions, and data repositories often need special patterns. The generator helps with common books and articles first. For odd sources, generate a close draft, then edit against the official example. Do not invent an author named "Admin" or "Staff" to fill a blank.

## Before you submit checklist

1. Style matches syllabus.
2. In-text citations match the list.
3. DOIs and URLs work when clicked.
4. Hanging indents and font match paper template.
5. Word count and grammar pass on the essay body using [Word Counter](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/word-counter) and [Grammar Checker](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/grammar-checker).
6. Reading level fits the assignment if [Reading Level Checker](https://www.freedailypro.com/tool/reading-level-checker) is part of your revision habit.

## Classroom honesty

Using a citation generator is normal professional practice. Submitting work you did not write is not. Keep generators in the formatting lane. Your analysis still has to be yours. When exams ban devices, you still need to know what a DOI is and how author-year works.

## Longer example walkthrough

Say your paper cites a 2021 journal article with three authors, a DOI, and pages 44–61. Enter all three authors in the order printed. Year 2021. Article title in sentence case for APA. Journal title in title case with italics per your editor. Volume, issue, page range, DOI. Generate. Paste. Compare to an APA journal example in the manual. Then cite (First Author et al., 2021) or the full author list in text as your edition requires for three authors. Mismatch between list and in-text is a common deduction.
