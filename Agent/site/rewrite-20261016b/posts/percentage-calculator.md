## Match the question to the mode

- Percent of: 18% of $240.
- What percent is A of B: 45 is what percent of 200.
- Percent change: from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.
Do not mix "percent of" with "percent change" when reporting to a client.

## Common mistakes

- Applying a discount percent to the wrong base (pre-tax vs post-tax).
- Calling a rise from 10 to 12 a 2% change instead of 20%.
- Stacking two discounts as if they were one combined percent without checking.
- Rounding mid-steps differently than the finance team.

## FAQ

**School vs business?** Same math; different labels. Always label the base.

**Compound discounts?** Calculate sequentially or as your policy defines—do not invent a single percent unless policy says so.

**Taxes?** Percent calculator can compute tax amounts when you know the rate; confirm local rules separately.

## Percent of a total

Retail discount: 20% of 150 is 30 off, 120 remaining before tax. Tip: 18% of the bill after tax or before tax depends on local habit—state your base. Sales commission: 7% of 12,000 is 840. Write the base next to the result every time so later readers know what you meant.

## Percent change for growth stories

From 200 units to 250 units is a 25% increase, not a 50 unit percent. From 250 back to 200 is a 20% decrease. Asymmetry surprises people who expect the reverse path to match. Percentage Calculator modes for change keep the formula consistent.

## Reverse problems

"45 is what percent of 180?" equals 25%. Students and analysts both miss this when they divide in the wrong order. State the sentence, then pick the mode that matches, then compute.

## Markups versus margins

These are not the same. Markup on cost and margin on price use different bases. If your business uses one term in meetings, compute with that definition and label it. Do not switch labels mid-report.

## Stacked discounts

Twenty percent off, then an extra ten percent off the reduced price, is not thirty percent off the original unless marketing says so with full clarity. Calculate sequentially if that is the policy. Customers notice when receipts disagree with ads.

## School weights

If projects are 40% of the grade and exams 60%, compute weighted pieces carefully. Percentage tools help, but the syllabus owns the weights. Show intermediate steps when teachers require work shown.

## Spreadsheet versus quick tool

Spreadsheets are fine for models. A dedicated percentage calculator is faster for one-off meeting math and less error-prone than rebuilding formulas under pressure. Use both where they fit.

## Rounding policy

Decide whether to round to cents, whole dollars, or two decimals for rates. Finance teams often have a policy. Apply it the same way every time. Mid-calculation rounding can drift totals.
