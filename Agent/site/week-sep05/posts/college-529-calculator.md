## Define the goal without fantasy

Are you targeting one year of public in-state costs, four years, or a private school sticker? Room and board or tuition only? Current dollars or a rough inflation adjustment? Write the assumption down. The calculator cannot know which story you mean.

Many families aim for half of projected costs and plan on aid, work, or loans for the rest. That is a valid strategy if it is conscious. The problem is accidental underfunding dressed as optimism.

## Growth rates and honesty

Investment returns are not guaranteed. Using a rosy rate makes the monthly number look small and friendly. Using a pessimistic rate makes people give up. Pick a moderate assumption and re-run with a lower one as a stress test. The point is a range, not a prophecy.

## A walkthrough pattern

1. Estimate total savings target in today's dollars for the share you want to cover.
2. Count years until the first tuition bill.
3. Enter a moderate annual return assumption.
4. Read the required monthly contribution.
5. Re-run with a lower return and a higher target.

If both monthly numbers are impossible, lower the target coverage percentage or extend the working timeline. Math does not care about pride.

## 529 specifics the tool does not replace

Tax treatment, state deductions, beneficiary rules, and qualified expense definitions are legal and tax topics. Use IRS and state resources or a qualified advisor for those. The calculator is for contribution sizing, not tax advice.

## Behavioral tips that matter more than basis points

Automate the contribution the day after payday. Increase it when income rises. Avoid treating the 529 as an emergency account. Review annually when tuition estimates update. Small process beats perfect spreadsheets abandoned in April.

## Limitations

The tool will not know your scholarship odds, your child's major, or whether a grandparent will gift tuition. It will tell you what monthly savings are required under the assumptions you typed. That is still more useful than a viral "save $200 forever" tip with no target attached.

Start with the 529 calculator, sanity-check against retirement and loan tools, and write the monthly number into the budget like any other non-negotiable bill you chose on purpose.
