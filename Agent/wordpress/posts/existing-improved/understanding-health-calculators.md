## BMI: useful screen, not a full story

BMI is weight divided by height squared — a formula from the 1830s built for population statistics, not individual assessment. It cannot tell muscle from fat. A muscular athlete and a sedentary person can share a BMI and have very different body composition. Treat BMI as a rough screening number, not a personal verdict.

## Body fat percentage estimates

Free body fat tools often use the US Navy method: a tape-measure formula with neck, waist, and (for women) hip measurements. It is a real improvement over BMI for composition, but it is still an estimate compared with clinical methods such as DEXA. Use it to track trends over time with consistent measurement technique, not as an absolute lab result.

## BMR and daily calorie estimates

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is roughly what your body burns at complete rest. Calorie calculators take BMR and multiply by an activity factor to estimate total daily energy needs. Those multipliers are averages. Your actual needs shift with sleep, stress, muscle mass, and health conditions. Free calculators give a starting range for planning — not a prescription.

## Activity factors and their limits

Calorie tools multiply BMR by activity levels such as sedentary, light, moderate, or very active. Those labels are coarse. Two people who both “sit at a desk” can differ by thousands of steps. Use the free estimate as a band, then adjust from energy, performance, and professional guidance — not from guilt about a single number.
