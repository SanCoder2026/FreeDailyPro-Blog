## Decoding Profit Margins: Gross Versus Net

At the heart of e-commerce financial management is the fundamental distinction between gross margin and net margin. Gross margin measures the difference between your revenue and the direct cost of goods sold (COGS). However, stopping at gross margin is a dangerous trap. Net profit takes into account every single operational expense, including platform subscription fees, transaction processing charges, advertising spend, warehousing overhead, customer acquisition costs, and return processing.

| Cost Component | Description | Impact on Profitability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** | Direct manufacturing, raw materials, and unit packaging | Establishes the absolute baseline floor for pricing |
| **Marketplace Fees** | Referral fees, closing fees, and monthly store subscriptions | Consumes between 8% to 20% of gross revenue |
| **Fulfillment & Shipping** | Pick, pack, postage, and dimensional weight charges | Often accounts for the single largest variable cost |
| **Marketing & Ads** | PPC campaigns, social media ads, and influencer partnerships | Directly dictates customer acquisition cost (CAC) |

When evaluating your pricing strategy, you must ensure that your margins absorb unexpected returns and chargebacks. Utilizing rigorous analytical tools ensures that you never price an item below its fully loaded cost.

## The Hidden Trap of Marketplace Fees and Shipping Math

Marketplace fees are notoriously multifaceted. Beyond standard transaction percentages, sellers must account for storage fees during peak seasons, long-term storage penalties, and inbound freight forwarding costs. Shipping math is equally treacherous. Carriers utilize dimensional weight (DIM weight) pricing, which bills you based on the physical space a package occupies rather than its actual scale weight. If you ship lightweight, bulky items in oversized corrugated boxes, your shipping margins will evaporate overnight.
